Tech stocks tumble, record $6.3bn Binance hack, and Iran tensions lift oil
Daily Briefing · Saturday, 31 October 2026

AT A GLANCE
- Wall Street had its worst session since June as AI-spending fears hit tech; Nasdaq fell 2.86%, S&P 500 fell 1.82%, and Intel had its worst day since the 1970s.
- Hackers linked to North Korea stole $6.3bn from Binance, the largest crypto theft on record; Binance says it will cover customer losses.
- Mines set four tankers ablaze in the Strait of Hormuz, intensifying the Iran war and pushing Brent crude up 2.40% to $117.64.
- The US approved a $2.1bn sale of Patriot missiles to Ukraine, one of the biggest steps in arms support since 2025.
- Poland deployed riot police after its president suspended the constitutional court's chief judge, deepening a rule-of-law standoff.

TOP STORIES
- AI spending fears trigger worst tech selloff since June: The selloff signals growing investor skepticism about the AI capital-spending boom that has powered the stock market's gains, and any further unwinding could ripple through broader indices and credit markets.
- North Korea-linked hackers steal record $6.3bn from Binance: The theft underscores persistent security vulnerabilities at crypto exchanges and highlights how crypto theft has become a major source of foreign currency for North Korea's isolated economy.
- Mine attacks set four tankers ablaze in Strait of Hormuz: The Strait of Hormuz carries a large share of global seaborne oil trade, and further attacks there could escalate the war and keep pushing up oil and freight prices.
- US approves $2.1bn Patriot missile sale to Ukraine: Patriot interceptors are described as the only effective weapon against Russian ballistic missiles, so the sale could affect Ukraine's ability to defend its cities, though the long delivery timeline limits any near-term impact.
- US midterm elections loom over jittery investors: A change in control of Congress could affect the direction of fiscal and regulatory policy for the rest of Trump's term, though markets are described as more focused on AI spending, rates and the Iran war.
- Fed officials signal possible return to rate hikes: A pivot back toward rate hikes would mark a reversal for markets that had been pricing in a less restrictive Fed, and could pressure both bond and equity prices.
- Riot police deployed as Poland's top court crisis escalates: The standoff deepens a rule-of-law dispute between Poland's president and government ahead of a parliamentary election next year, and highlights ongoing tensions over judicial independence within the EU.
- Canada and EU unveil new alliance as old order unravels: The initiative signals a broader realignment among traditional US allies as confidence in the transatlantic relationship under Trump weakens, with potential implications for trade and diplomatic ties involving the EU.
- No clear winner in Israel election: A prolonged coalition-formation process in Israel could add uncertainty to the country's political direction amid ongoing regional conflicts.
- BP, Chevron and Exxon post bumper profits on war-driven refining margins: The results show how the Ukraine and Iran conflicts are redistributing profits toward oil majors through higher refining margins, even as higher fuel costs weigh on consumers and other businesses.
