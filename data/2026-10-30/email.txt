Tech-led Wall Street selloff spreads to Asia as Binance hack, Israel vote add pressure
Daily Briefing · Friday, 30 October 2026

AT A GLANCE
- Wall Street's AI-driven tech selloff deepened after Alphabet's historic $500bn drop, Amazon's cash burn and Intel's guidance miss; Asian and European markets diverged sharply overnight.
- Binance suffered a record $6.3bn security breach, with North Korea-linked hackers blamed; Bitcoin fell and about $3bn in assets were frozen or recovered.
- Israel's election left Netanyahu's bloc and the opposition both short of a majority, setting up weeks of coalition bargaining.
- ECB President Christine Lagarde said she will step down nine months early to speak freely on world affairs.
- The Bank of Japan held rates, declaring its 2% inflation target met for the first time since 2013; the yen strengthened.

TOP STORIES
- AI spending fears deepen Wall Street's tech-stock rout: The results are the clearest signal yet that investors are questioning whether heavy AI infrastructure spending by the largest tech companies will pay off, a question now weighing on markets well beyond the US.
- Binance hit by biggest crypto hack on record, $6.3bn affected: A breach of this scale at the leading exchange raises fresh questions about security across the crypto industry and comes as North Korean state-linked groups continue to target digital-asset platforms for funding.
- Israel's election leaves no governing majority: The deadlock prolongs political uncertainty in Israel and gives smaller and Arab-led parties unusual leverage over who ultimately governs.
- ECB's Lagarde to step down nine months early: Her early exit sets up a leadership contest at the eurozone's central bank at a sensitive moment for European politics and ahead of a French election.
- Bank of Japan holds rates, says inflation target finally met: Confirmation that Japan has durably exited deflation raises expectations of a rate increase as soon as December, a shift markets will watch closely given Japan's role in global funding markets.
- US economy grew 3.4% in the third quarter: Strong headline growth complicates the picture for policymakers and voters alike, since it sits alongside weak sentiment and a stock market rattled by AI spending concerns.
- Localized Ukraine truce holds as Pentagon urges Europe to tone down Russia rhetoric: The mixed signals, a local truce alongside continued strikes elsewhere, and reported US pressure on European allies, point to unresolved tension over how the war and any peace process should proceed.
- North Korea pushes drone and missile buildup: Pyongyang's weapons push and alleged involvement in a major cyber theft add to security concerns in the region and point to new sanctions-evading revenue streams for the regime.
- Spain's train crash death toll rises to 69: A second major rail disaster in a year raises pressure on Spain's government over infrastructure safety just as the country heads toward a national election.
- Trump cancels midterm rallies as Alaska Senate dispute escalates: Legal disputes over a potentially pivotal Senate race, combined with questions about Trump's global political influence, add uncertainty to a midterm election that could determine control of Congress.
