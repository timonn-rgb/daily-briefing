Analysis

US stocks rose Wednesday after data showed core PCE inflation, the Fed's preferred gauge, rose 0.2% in August, with the annual core rate at 3.0%, both below the roughly 3.3% forecast, while consumer spending picked up sharply. Separate ADP data showed private payrolls up 90,000 in September, better than expected. The S&P 500 climbed 0.65% to 7,720.40 and the Nasdaq Composite gained 1.09%, while the Dow rose 0.18%. Treasury yields eased, with the 10-year at 5.24%, retreating from the near-25-year high hit earlier in the week, and the VIX fell to 15.74. Traders trimmed bets on a Fed rate hike in October.

Asian markets ended higher, led by the Nikkei 225's 1.94% jump, with the Hang Seng and Shanghai Composite also advancing. European bourses traded lower, with the DAX down 0.54%, the Euro Stoxx 50 down 0.55% and the FTSE 100 down 0.21%, as accelerating inflation across the eurozone's biggest economies added pressure on the ECB and bond investors continued digesting warnings over French creditworthiness. WTI crude rose 1.63% while Brent fell 4.05%; gold gained 0.76% and bitcoin rose 1.13%.

Later Wednesday, Micron reports quarterly earnings after the close, the main US corporate event on an otherwise quiet calendar for central-bank decisions.

World news

Geopolitics

FlyDubai flight diverted after pilot allegedly stabs co-pilot

A FlyDubai flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv made an emergency landing in Saudi Arabia after one pilot stabbed the other and allegedly attempted to crash the aircraft, according to Israeli officials and passenger accounts. Both pilots were hospitalized, and a passenger described chaos and blood in the cockpit. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the incident is being investigated as a possible attempted terror attack and that Israel is preparing for other potential threats. The motive has not been established.

Why it matters: The incident raises fresh security concerns for air travel to and from Israel and comes as Netanyahu's government faces renewed scrutiny over attack preparedness.

Sources: Reuters · CNBC Top News · Guardian World · NYT World · NPR World · Guardian World · AP News · NYT World · France 24

Last US troops leave Iraq after 23-year mission

The United States completed the withdrawal of its remaining troops from Iraq, ending a military presence that began with the 2003 invasion and continued through a 12-year coalition mission against the Islamic State group. Iraqi forces held parades in Baghdad and Najaf to mark what the government called 'Sovereignty Day.' A retired US general who commanded troops in Iraq warned the exit could give Iran and the Islamic State more room to operate. Reactions inside Iraq were mixed, with some celebrating the end of the American presence and others voicing concern about what follows.

Why it matters: The withdrawal removes a long-standing US military presence from a country positioned between Washington and Tehran, with implications for regional stability and Iran's influence.

Sources: NYT World · NYT World · BBC World · France 24 · France 24 · NPR World · NPR World · NPR World

Russia hits Ukraine's energy grid, warns NATO over Kaliningrad

Russia launched its largest attack on Ukraine's energy infrastructure since spring, striking 11 regions overnight and hitting Kyiv's data centers, communications infrastructure and power grid; Ukrainian officials said at least four people were killed. Separately, the Kremlin threatened nuclear strikes against NATO countries, accusing the alliance of preparing to blockade Kaliningrad, Russia's Baltic exclave. NATO condemned what it called Moscow's 'irresponsible nuclear rhetoric.' Russia also extended its ban on most diesel exports through the end of October.

Why it matters: Escalating attacks on Ukrainian infrastructure alongside direct nuclear threats against NATO members mark a sharp rise in tensions between Russia and the Western alliance.

Sources: CNBC Top News · BBC World · AP News · FT World · Reuters · Reuters · FT World · Reuters

MI5 accuses Chinese institute of spying on UK AI research

Britain's domestic security service MI5 issued an alert accusing a Chinese state-owned conglomerate, the China General Technology Research Institute, of using a front company to fund and access British university research on artificial intelligence. MI5 said the institute has 'very strong ties' to Chinese intelligence and urged universities to cut ties with it. The alert is among the more unusual public espionage warnings the agency has issued.

Why it matters: The warning highlights growing UK concern about Chinese access to sensitive AI research through academic funding, at a time when universities rely heavily on international grants.

Sources: Guardian World · FT World · Bloomberg Politics · Guardian World

Iran's nuclear program enters uncertain phase amid US diplomacy

Iran received a US response to its proposal following recent talks in Qatar, though President Trump denied reports that the US had offered sanctions relief in exchange for nuclear concessions. Iran's nuclear program remains opaque as Tehran debates withdrawing from the nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty, with satellite imagery showing activity at known nuclear sites. Iran also told its public that Trump's war has failed. Crude flows through the Strait of Hormuz have recovered, though tanker insurance costs and geopolitical risk remain elevated.

Why it matters: A potential Iranian exit from the Non-Proliferation Treaty and stalled US-Iran diplomacy keep alive risks to regional stability and oil-shipping routes through the Strait of Hormuz.

Sources: DW · CNBC Top News · Reuters · Reuters · AP News · Al Jazeera

Economy & Policy

Cooler US inflation lifts Wall Street, eases rate-hike bets

The core PCE price index, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, rose 0.2% in August, and annual core inflation came in at 3.0%, well below the roughly 3.3% forecast economists had expected. US consumer spending rose in August at its fastest pace in over a year. Separate ADP data showed private-sector employment increased by 90,000 in September, better than expected. Traders pared back bets on a Federal Reserve rate hike in October, and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite advanced.

Why it matters: Weaker-than-expected inflation reduces the likelihood of a near-term Fed rate hike, a key driver of Wednesday's equity rally and lower Treasury yields.

Sources: CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · Reuters · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Markets

Eurozone inflation accelerates, bond markets stay on edge after French warning

Inflation jumped across the eurozone's biggest economies in September, adding pressure on the European Central Bank to consider raising rates. Vanguard warned that France is 'degrading credit' as its borrowing costs surge, with fiscal pressures compounded by the recent Iran conflict and an ongoing budget standoff. Global government bonds are on track for their worst quarter since 2024 as elevated oil prices revive inflation concerns. German unemployment and state-level inflation both rose in September.

Why it matters: Rising eurozone inflation and deteriorating fiscal signals from France add pressure on European bond markets and the ECB's policy path just as US inflation data eased.

Sources: Reuters · FT World · FT World · Bloomberg Markets · Reuters · FT World

Business & Tech

AI safety remains unresolved after Trump's White House summit

Following President Trump's meeting with AI industry leaders at the White House, the sector remains largely unsettled on questions of safety, with tech bosses signing a 'morally binding' but non-regulatory commitment to self-police. Trump has pushed to rename artificial intelligence 'Super Intelligence' even as researchers and companies question the shift. Bank of England Governor Andrew Bailey said authorities need the 'right to intervene' in the AI industry given growing risks, though he said regulation is 'not the right place to start.' The central bank separately flagged AI and debt as growing risks to financial stability.

Why it matters: Continued uncertainty over AI oversight, paired with explicit central-bank warnings about financial-stability risk, leaves regulatory and systemic-risk questions open for a sector attracting enormous investment.

Sources: CNBC Top News · BBC World · France 24 · BBC Business · Guardian Business · Reuters

Other

Manchester City found guilty of financial rule breaches

The Premier League found Manchester City guilty of breaching financial rules over nearly a decade, ruling that the club used 'sham' commercial contracts to inflate revenue and understate costs by more than £900 million. The club was found guilty of virtually all of the 115 charges brought against it. Manchester City called the case a 'conspiracy theory' and denies wrongdoing. The club could also be ordered to pay the Premier League up to £50 million in legal costs.

Why it matters: The verdict is one of the largest financial governance cases in English soccer history and could bring significant sporting and financial sanctions against one of the league's dominant clubs.

Sources: CNBC Top News · DW · Guardian Business · Al Jazeera · France 24 · AP News
