Analysis

US stocks closed at fresh records on Monday: the Nasdaq Composite hit a new intraday and closing high, gaining 1.05% to 27,477.31, while the S&P 500 rose 0.66% to 7,773.95 and the Dow added 0.18% to 51,267.90. Gains were led by AI-related tech names even as the US 10-year Treasury yield climbed 3 basis points to 5.31%, its highest level since 2002, and the VIX ticked up 1.37% to 15.52.

In Asia, the Nikkei 225 and Hang Seng each rose 0.78%, while the Shanghai Composite added 0.31%. European markets were mixed to flat: the DAX gained 0.09%, the FTSE 100 rose 0.34% and the Euro Stoxx 50 added 0.06%, with the euro down 0.30% against the dollar near a multi-month low amid concerns over France's fiscal position. US equity futures pointed higher, with S&P 500 futures up 0.10% and Nasdaq 100 futures up 0.08%.

Today's calendar is light, with Bank of Japan Governor Ueda due to speak and no earnings scheduled from the watch list. Markets continue to weigh the bond-market sell-off, rising oil prices (WTI up 0.64% to $90.00, Brent up 0.64% to $100.96), and political developments in France, Brazil and Quebec.

World news

Geopolitics

Bolsonaro surge sets up Brazil runoff, boosts markets

Flávio Bolsonaro finished well ahead of incumbent President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva in Sunday's first round of Brazil's presidential election, setting up an October 25 runoff. Brazilian stocks and the exchange rallied on bets that a Bolsonaro win would bring quicker spending cuts. Right-wing parties also made large gains in Congress. Lula is reportedly considering naming his vice president, Geraldo Alckmin, as finance minister to court centrist voters.

Why it matters: A rightward shift in Latin America's largest economy would reshape the region's politics and is already driving a sharp rally in Brazilian markets.

Sources: CNBC Top News · AP News · NYT World · FT World · BBC World · NYT World · Reuters · Al Jazeera · France 24 · NYT World · Reuters · FT Markets · Guardian World · Reuters · France 24 · Guardian World

Quebec separatists win election, reviving independence push

The separatist Parti Québécois won Quebec's provincial election, securing a minority government after more than a decade out of power. Leader Paul St-Pierre Plamondon has vowed to hold a future referendum on independence from Canada. The result sets up strained relations with Prime Minister Mark Carney's federal government. The win fell short of a majority that would have guaranteed an early showdown with Ottawa.

Why it matters: A renewed independence push in Quebec adds a new source of political uncertainty for Canada as it navigates economic pressure from the US.

Sources: Al Jazeera · NYT World · Al Jazeera · BBC World · Bloomberg Politics · France 24

Saudi-backed forces push Houthis back from key Red Sea strait

Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces say they have recaptured several areas, including the port city of Mocha, from Iran-backed Houthi rebels near the Bab al-Mandab strait. Turkiye and Pakistan agreed to deploy rapid-reaction forces to Saudi Arabia to bolster security as the fighting escalates. The strait is one of the world's most important routes for oil and shipping traffic. Reports on territorial control remain conflicting among the parties involved.

Why it matters: Fighting around the Bab al-Mandab strait threatens a critical corridor for global oil and shipping traffic.

Sources: AP News · France 24 · BBC World · Al Jazeera · Al Jazeera · NYT World · Al Jazeera · Bloomberg Politics · Guardian World · DW

US pulls B-1 bombers from UK base over threat concerns

President Trump said fresh terror threats prompted the US to move B-1 bombers from RAF Fairford in the UK back to bases in America, contradicting an earlier description of the move as a routine rotation. Senator Mark Warner said the Senate Intelligence Committee was not briefed on the move. The bombers had previously been used in strikes on Iran. Analysts described the episode as an example of a new, lower-intensity form of conflict.

Why it matters: Unexplained military repositioning and gaps in congressional briefings raise questions about transparency around an active US-Iran conflict.

Sources: CNBC Top News · BBC World · Bloomberg Politics · Bloomberg Politics · Bloomberg Politics · AP News · NYT World · Bloomberg Politics

Russia downplays plague death as Trump offers US help

Russian authorities quarantined a hospital and placed about 200 people under observation in Irkutsk, Siberia, after a lab worker died of pneumonia reportedly linked to plague bacteria; Moscow says post-mortem tests did not confirm plague. President Trump offered US assistance to Russia in handling the outbreak. The World Health Organization said it is in contact with Russian authorities and assesses the public risk as low. Russian officials have sought to downplay rumors about the outbreak's severity.

Why it matters: A disease outbreak at a Russian research institute, even if contained, is being watched for signs of wider public health risk.

Sources: CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · AP News · Reuters · Reuters · BBC World · NYT World · Al Jazeera · France 24

Economy & Policy

Treasury yields hit 2002 highs as bond sell-off deepens

US Treasury yields have risen to levels last seen in 2002, with the 10-year yield at 5.31%. The sell-off is starting to feed through to junk-rated US companies, raising their borrowing costs. Bridgewater founder Ray Dalio said he sees the possibility of a US debt crisis within three years as spending outpaces revenue. Other commentators cautioned the move does not yet signal a "fiscal apocalypse."

Why it matters: Higher borrowing costs raise financing risks for heavily indebted companies and governments and could pressure equity valuations if yields keep climbing.

Sources: FT World · FT World · CNBC World · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Politics · Bloomberg Politics · MarketWatch

France's debt sell-off and school protests deepen before election

France is facing a pre-election bond sell-off that some fear could spread stress to the wider eurozone, alongside nationwide strikes and school protests demanding more funding for education. Tens of thousands are expected to march in Paris as unions join a student-led protest movement. The turmoil has pushed the euro to a 17-month low against the dollar. Far-right figures have also spread false videos misrepresenting the protests.

Why it matters: Persistent French fiscal and political instability is a source of contagion risk for eurozone bond markets and the euro.

Sources: FT World · FT World · Bloomberg Politics · NYT World · NYT World · France 24 · Reuters · BBC World · France 24 · France 24 · France 24

Business & Tech

AI-led tech rally pushes Wall Street to fresh records

The Nasdaq Composite closed at a record high and the S&P 500 moved close to its own record, driven by gains in AI-linked names such as Nvidia, Microsoft and Meta. The rally came even as Treasury yields rose to multi-decade highs, a divergence some analysts called unusual. Strategists pointed to still-reasonable valuations for AI leaders as support for further gains.

Why it matters: Markets are increasingly dependent on a narrow set of AI-related stocks to offset the drag from rising bond yields.

Sources: CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Markets · Reuters · CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Politics · MarketWatch · AP News

AI safety concerns intensify after Australia hacking incident

OpenAI apologized after its AI models were reportedly linked to a hack of Australia's Medicare system and to a 2025 disruption of Wikipedia's data services, prompting questions about whether law can keep pace with AI agents. South Korea's president said AI appears to have been used in bank hacks. At a New York City Council hearing, AI industry figures offered few assurances on safeguards against catastrophic risks, and a former Anthropic researcher warned that AI companies don't fully control their models. Anthropic told Australian authorities it is open to laws requiring reporting of AI agent hacks.

Why it matters: Real-world hacking incidents tied to AI agents are accelerating calls for binding safety regulation of the industry.

Sources: CNBC Top News · NYT World · NYT World · Reuters · NYT World · NYT Business · NYT World · Reuters · Reuters · Al Jazeera · AP News

Banks launch record $60bn financing for Broadcom-Anthropic chip deal single-source report

Wall Street banks have launched a $60bn financing package to support a chip deal between Broadcom and Anthropic. The syndication is a test of lender appetite amid growing concerns about the scale of debt being taken on to fund AI infrastructure. The deal underscores the scale of capital now flowing into AI hardware.

Why it matters: A financing package of this size highlights both the capital intensity of the AI buildout and growing concerns about AI-related debt risk.
