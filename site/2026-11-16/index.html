Analysis

The S&P 500 fell 0.40% and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.91% in the last US session, while the Dow Jones rose 0.10%. The VIX volatility index climbed 3.21% and the US 10-year yield rose 5 basis points to 5.15%, reflecting investor caution around the AI trade. Among S&P 500 movers, AMD fell 8.8%, Dell dropped 7.2% and Cisco lost 6.2%, while Charter Communications, Steris, Global Payments, Fidelity National Information Services and Fox Corporation each gained more than 4%.

Asian markets extended the pullback today: the Nikkei 225 fell 2.34% and the Shanghai Composite lost 0.78%, while the Hang Seng rose 0.80%. The slide followed weaker-than-expected Chinese October data on industrial output, retail sales and investment, and a surprise contraction in Japan's third-quarter GDP. In Europe, the DAX, FTSE 100 and Euro Stoxx 50 were little changed in the last session, but US stock futures point to a lower open, with S&P 500 futures down 0.55% and Nasdaq 100 futures down 1.16%.

Today's calendar features a speech from Fed Chairman Warsh at 18:00 Berlin time, flagged as high impact, with no earnings due from the watch list. Markets remain focused on Nvidia's upcoming earnings and Anthropic's IPO this week as tests of confidence in the AI trade. Gold slipped 1.18% and the dollar firmed slightly, while WTI crude edged up 0.12% and Brent eased 0.14%.

Geopolitics

Trump, Xi and Putin head to Asia for high-stakes summits

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin are converging on Asia this week for summits in China and the Philippines, in what is being described as an unprecedented meeting of the three leaders; talks are expected to cover nuclear non-proliferation, the Middle East war, trade policy and possibly the Ukraine war. Ahead of the summit, the US and nine partner nations, including India, Singapore and the UK, signed a pledge to safeguard sensitive AI technology and infrastructure amid competition with China. Canada's Mark Carney, India's Narendra Modi and the Philippines' Ferdinand Marcos Jr. are also attending regional summits, with several leaders seeking to deepen trade ties as a hedge against US protectionism. ASEAN leaders meeting ahead of the summit failed to agree on a South China Sea code of conduct and denounced violence in Myanmar.

Why it matters: The summit brings together leaders of the world's largest economies and military powers at a moment of trade tension and ongoing wars in Ukraine and the Middle East, with outcomes likely to shape global trade policy and AI-technology competition with China.

Houthis advance in Yemen as government forces retreat

Forces aligned with Yemen's government and Saudi Arabia are retreating as Iran-backed Houthi forces advance, with Saudi-backed troops moving from the Bab al-Mandab Strait to defend the interim capital, Aden. Analysts said momentum in the civil war is tilting toward the Houthis, who have taken control of the key Red Sea waterway. Iran separately struck Bahrain. The Houthis have unilaterally released 600 war detainees.

Why it matters: Houthi control of the Bab al-Mandab Strait affects one of the world's busiest shipping routes, with implications for global trade and shipping costs through the Red Sea.

Sources: Reuters · CNBC Top News · Al Jazeera · France 24 · BBC World · DW · NYT World · Al Jazeera

Estonia to close Russia border crossings after sabotage attack

Estonia said it will close all its border crossings with Russia from 1 December, after Estonian authorities said Russia's intelligence service ordered a sabotage attack on a drone maker in Viimsi. Estonia's prime minister linked the attack to Tallinn's support for Ukraine.

Why it matters: The closure marks an escalation in tensions between a NATO and EU member state and Russia, adding to concerns about hybrid warfare in the Baltic region.

Sources: Guardian World · Al Jazeera · Reuters · DW

Economy & Policy

China's economic slowdown deepens in October

China's economic activity slowed more than expected in October, with industrial output, retail sales and investment all missing estimates, according to official data. BofA's Helen Qiao said the figures could signal a need for further policy easing, even as the People's Bank of China withdrew medium-term funds from the financial system, a sign of caution toward easing bets. Analysts said a deepening reliance on external demand leaves China more exposed to shocks from abroad as domestic momentum fades. Beijing has pushed to speed up budget spending to offset the weaker growth.

Why it matters: Weaker Chinese demand is already weighing on commodity markets and regional trade partners, and comes as Beijing's stimulus measures have yet to visibly lift investment.

Sources: CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Markets · FT World · Bloomberg Markets · Reuters · Bloomberg Markets

Japan's economy unexpectedly contracts in the third quarter

Japan's economy unexpectedly contracted in the third quarter, shrinking at an annualised rate of 0.1% in the July-September period. Weak consumer spending, record rains and elevated energy costs — after household energy subsidies expired in September — were cited as factors. The contraction complicates the Bank of Japan's plans on interest rates.

Why it matters: A surprise contraction in the world's fourth-largest economy adds a complication for policymakers at a time when growth signals from China are also weakening.

Sources: CNBC Top News · FT World · Reuters · Reuters

Brussels to propose €45bn Ukraine loan single-source report

The European Commission is due to propose a €45bn loan for Ukraine on Wednesday, borrowed against the EU budget, to help bridge Kyiv's funding gap for next year.

Why it matters: The loan plan addresses a near-term funding shortfall for Ukraine's war effort and government, a decision that requires agreement from EU member states.

Sources: FT World

Business & Tech

Nvidia leadership crisis deepens as pressure mounts for Huang's return

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned last week after revelations of two extramarital affairs, one of them with a woman, Alice Huang, who has been accused of being a Chinese spy and was linked to the firm Megaspeed; Megaspeed says she left the company in August 2025 and calls itself a 'legitimate and lawful business.' US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent gave Nvidia's board 10 days to demonstrate compliance with US export controls and called for an outsider to lead the company, while Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick faces scrutiny over his sons' financial ties to the chipmaker. More than 165,000 people, including Elon Musk, have signed an open letter calling for Huang's reinstatement, and the Financial Times reported he is discussing a comeback with allies, a report that steadied Nvidia's shares. Colette Kress is currently leading the company.

Why it matters: The leadership vacuum at a company central to the AI trade, combined with scrutiny of US export-control compliance, adds uncertainty ahead of Nvidia's earnings this week, a report widely seen as pivotal for sentiment toward AI-related stocks.

Sources: CNBC Top News · BBC World · CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · Reuters · NYT Business · FT World · Al Jazeera · Guardian World · Guardian World · Bloomberg Markets · France 24 · Al Jazeera · Reuters

Anthropic's IPO tests appetite for the AI trade

Anthropic's IPO is landing this week as the AI industry looks for a bright spot after recent volatility, with its reception described as having outsized importance for a tech sector heavily invested in the AI lab. Asian shares fell on Monday as traders braced for both Nvidia's earnings and the Anthropic IPO, seen as tests of the broader tech rally. US stock futures also slipped ahead of the events.

Why it matters: The IPO's reception will be read as a signal of investor appetite for AI-related assets at a moment when AI stocks have been under pressure.

Sources: CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Markets · Reuters

Hong Kong developer New World's shares tumble 47% single-source report

Shares in Hong Kong property developer New World fell 47% after the company announced a plan to raise funds via a $3.2bn rights issue, offering new stock at an 80% discount, as it moves to reduce its debts.

Why it matters: The sharp share-price drop highlights ongoing stress among heavily indebted Chinese property developers.

Hundreds of thousands protest in Prague over media funding plan

An estimated 350,000 people rallied in Prague against a government plan to scrap the licence fee funding public broadcasters and replace it with state-budget funding. Critics, including a prominent Czech TV presenter who said he would leave his job over the changes, argue the plan threatens media independence, while the government says broadcasters need to cut costs.

Why it matters: The size of the protest signals significant public resistance to a change that critics say could give the government more influence over public media in an EU member state.
