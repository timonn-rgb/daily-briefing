Analysis

US equities closed higher for a third straight session on Wednesday, with the S&P 500 up 0.58%, the Dow up 0.69% and the Nasdaq Composite up 1.05%, helped by Tesla and SpaceX's earnings-driven rally and a revenue beat from Meta. Beneath the index gains, some AI-linked names came under pressure: CoreWeave shares extended an October slide to -29% as bond spreads on its debt widened, amid renewed doubts about the durability of the AI trade. The VIX eased to 16.68 and the US 10-year yield rose 3bp to 5.15%.

European markets closed higher Wednesday (DAX +1.35%, Euro Stoxx 50 +1.40%, FTSE 100 +0.19%), though French bond yields climbed to a 24-year high above 5% as investors weighed the fallout from last weekend's unrest. In Asia on Thursday, the Nikkei 225 fell 2.06%, the Hang Seng 2.87% and the Shanghai Composite 0.89%, with mainland China's CSI 300 falling into bear-market territory and Alibaba posting its steepest drop in two months.

US futures point to a lower open (S&P 500 futures -0.69%, Nasdaq 100 futures -1.34%) as investors digest Tesla's and Meta's results alongside escalating Gulf tensions, which pushed WTI crude up 1.59% to $98.69 and Brent up 1.93%. Gold fell 1.05% while the dollar index was little changed. Procter & Gamble reports earnings today, with no other items on the economic calendar.

World news

Geopolitics

US pulls diplomats from Saudi Arabia as Houthi attacks raise fears of wider Gulf conflict

The US ordered non-emergency staff and families of government employees out of Saudi Arabia after Houthi strikes hit King Fahd airport and Saudi Aramco's Shaybah oilfield, killing one person and injuring others. Similar withdrawals were reported in the UAE, Jordan, Qatar and Bahrain. The State Department cited increasing Houthi hostilities against Saudi Arabia. Saudi Arabia's capital airport later reopened to limited traffic.

Why it matters: A widening conflict involving Gulf oil infrastructure threatens energy supply routes and has already pushed oil prices higher, which could feed through to global inflation.

Sources: CNBC Top News · Guardian World · DW · AP News · BBC World · Reuters · Al Jazeera · Reuters · FT Markets · AP News · Al Jazeera

Russia agrees to resume Ukraine talks, but missiles still hit Kyiv overnight

The Kremlin said it is ready to resume three-way talks on the Ukraine war with Kyiv and Washington after US envoy Steve Witkoff met Vladimir Putin in St Petersburg. During the visit, Putin ordered the release of American Stephen Hubbard, jailed in Russia for over four years. Hours after the talks, Russia and Ukraine exchanged strikes, with Russian missiles wounding six people in Kyiv. Ukraine's Zelenskiy said he hopes for new talks with Russia and the US by early November.

Why it matters: Renewed diplomatic contact raises hopes of de-escalation, but continued strikes on both capitals show fighting is still intensifying even as talks are discussed.

Sources: AP News · France 24 · DW · France 24 · Guardian World · Al Jazeera · Reuters · Reuters · BBC World · Reuters · AP News · Al Jazeera

Drone strike kills more than 150 in Sudan's civil war, UN warns of wider crisis

A drone strike in Abyei killed more than 150 people as Sudan's warring sides increasingly rely on drones to strike across long distances, inflicting heavy civilian tolls. The UN held an emergency international meeting on the escalating crisis and warned of a wider regional toll from the conflict.

Why it matters: Rising civilian casualties and growing use of drones signal an escalation that the UN says risks destabilizing the broader region.

Sources: NYT World · France 24

Economy & Policy

China's stock market slides into bear territory as tech selloff deepens

China's CSI 300 index has fallen 20% from its June peak, entering bear-market territory after a ten-week rout, as the unwinding of the global AI trade and a lack of new stimulus weigh on sentiment ahead of earnings season. Alibaba shares posted their steepest two-month drop after Chinese authorities summoned e-commerce operators over product-quality and consumer-protection standards. Asian markets broadly fell on Thursday as investors weighed tech earnings and Gulf tensions.

Why it matters: A deepening selloff in the world's second-largest economy, combined with fading AI-trade enthusiasm, adds to pressure on global risk appetite.

Sources: CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Markets · Reuters

French bond yields hit 24-year high as unrest and budget worries persist

French bond yields topped 5%, their highest level since 2002, after widespread protests and violence over the weekend left investors questioning the government's finances. Paris banned further protests in parts of the city, and President Macron accused Donald Trump and other foreign leaders of making "stupid comments" about the unrest. A researcher group said pro-Russian accounts had amplified anger online ahead of the riots.

Why it matters: Rising borrowing costs for one of the eurozone's largest economies, tied to political and fiscal instability, could spill over into wider European bond and currency markets.

Sources: France 24 · France 24 · DW · Guardian World · France 24

AI-trade jitters resurface as CoreWeave credit stress and haven demand grow

CoreWeave's market confidence eroded further as its shares fell 29% in October and credit default swap spreads on its debt widened. Long-end Treasury yields touched a one-month low as renewed doubts about the durability of the AI trade pushed investors toward havens. Copper also fell as the broader tech-stock selloff dulled risk appetite for industrial metals.

Why it matters: Stress in a key AI-infrastructure financier is an early signal of how sensitive markets have become to any crack in the AI investment narrative.

Sources: CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Politics

Business & Tech

Meta fined $25 billion in New Mexico over Cambridge Analytica-era privacy case

A New Mexico judge ordered Meta to pay $25 billion after a jury found the company deceived users about data privacy in a case linked to the Cambridge Analytica scandal. The penalty is larger than Meta's prior $18 billion settlement with 48 other states over similar claims, and is described as the largest such fine to date. Meta said it plans to contest the ruling.

Why it matters: The record fine raises the financial and legal stakes for Meta over how it handles user data, and could influence other pending privacy litigation against the company.

Sources: Reuters · CNBC Top News · BBC World · AP News · France 24 · BBC World

Meta beats Q3 estimates, bets on Muse AI assistant and creates new share class

Meta's third-quarter revenue topped estimates and the company issued strong guidance, with CEO Mark Zuckerberg saying accounts for its Muse AI assistant have surpassed 10 million. Investors focused on Meta's roadmap to monetize Muse beyond its core advertising business. Separately, Meta created a new class of stock partly to help fund AI investment while also addressing concerns about a California wealth tax that could have pushed Zuckerberg to relocate.

Why it matters: A credible new revenue line beyond advertising, paired with a large AI investment push, could reshape how investors value Meta relative to other AI-spending megacaps.

Sources: CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · AP News · MarketWatch · Reuters · Reuters · MarketWatch

Tesla and SpaceX beat earnings as Musk confirms merger talks

Tesla reported better-than-expected third-quarter results even as profit fell and auto revenue declined, while SpaceX reported surging revenue growth. Elon Musk confirmed that special committees at both companies are examining a possible merger that could consolidate his rocket, car and AI holdings into a company worth more than $3 trillion. Some commentators questioned whether the deal would benefit Tesla shareholders as much as Musk personally.

Why it matters: A merger of this scale would be among the largest in history and would concentrate an unusual share of global rocket, EV and AI capacity under one person's control.

Sources: CNBC Top News · BBC World · NYT Business · Nikkei Asia · CNBC Top News · AP News · MarketWatch

Other

Critical PHP security flaw triggers global website shutdowns

A newly discovered vulnerability in PHP, a programming language used across roughly 70% of websites, prompted precautionary takedowns of sites in multiple countries including Belgium and the Netherlands. The flaws were uncovered using AI and described as among the most significant security issues ever found. Google, OpenAI and Amazon were among the companies responding to the threat, and government security agencies urged businesses to patch immediately.

Why it matters: A flaw affecting the majority of the world's websites creates a broad window for exploitation by bad actors until systems are patched.
