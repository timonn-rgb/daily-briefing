Analysis

US stocks rose on Monday, with the S&P 500 up 0.70%, the Nasdaq Composite up 0.82% and the Dow Jones up 0.28%, driven by renewed optimism in the AI trade after Anthropic's IPO filing, which also lifted optical and chip-related names such as Lumentum. The VIX fell 5.45% to 18.39, and the 10-year Treasury yield held near 4.99%.

In Asia on Tuesday, the Nikkei 225 rose 1.56% as chip-linked stocks tracked the AI rally, while the Hang Seng fell 0.57% and the Shanghai Composite fell 0.37%. European benchmarks closed higher on Monday, with the DAX up 0.91%, the FTSE 100 up 0.72% and the Euro Stoxx 50 up 0.79%. Oil fell further, with Brent down 5.14% and WTI down 0.87%, as diplomatic efforts around Middle East conflicts continued.

Today, US index futures are little changed, with S&P 500 futures up 0.01% and Nasdaq 100 futures down 0.22%. No German economic data is scheduled, but earnings are due from Visa and Coca-Cola. The Bank of Japan is expected to hold rates this week while signaling a hawkish tilt toward a hike in December or January, and the UK's Chancellor Healey delivers his first budget on Wednesday. Gold held near $4,023.80 ahead of this week's Federal Reserve rate decision.

World news

Geopolitics

Israelis vote in first election since the October 7 attacks

Israel held a general election on Tuesday, its first since the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack and the wars in Gaza, Lebanon and Iran that followed. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's coalition faced a tight race against an opposition bloc led by former military chief Gadi Eisenkot, with most polling suggesting his coalition could lose its majority. Arab parties were projected to play a kingmaker role. The vote is widely described as one of the most consequential in Israel's history.

Why it matters: The result will determine whether Netanyahu remains in power after three years of regional war and could shape Israel's policy toward Iran, Gaza and the West Bank.

Sources: DW · NYT World · FT World · Bloomberg Politics · NPR World · Al Jazeera · NYT World · Reuters

Lula wins Brazil's presidency; currency suffers worst day in 25 years

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth term as Brazil's president, defeating Flávio Bolsonaro in a runoff after trailing in the first round and in prediction markets. Brazil's currency and stocks plunged, with the real recording its worst single-day drop in more than 25 years amid investor concern about fiscal management under Lula. Bolsonaro's Liberal Party conceded, but Brazil's Supreme Court banned him from leaving the country and ordered him to surrender his passport over a separate money-laundering investigation. World leaders congratulated Lula on the win.

Why it matters: The result bucks a rightward political shift across Latin America and raises market concern about Brazil's fiscal trajectory, while denying Trump a regional ally.

Sources: FT World · CNBC Top News · DW · NYT World · Bloomberg Politics · AP News · FT Markets · BBC World · NYT Business

Local Ukraine ceasefire begins as UN says Russian strikes were war crimes

A localized ceasefire brokered by US First Lady Melania Trump began in the Russian-occupied Ukrainian town of Oleshky, where about 2,000 people have been stranded amid shortages of food and medicine, to allow evacuations and aid deliveries. Russia continued attacks elsewhere, including a strike on Kyiv that killed one person and injured 10. Separately, a UN-backed panel said Russia's strikes on Ukraine's energy grid, which cut off heat and power on cold days, amounted to war crimes and were intended to cause civilian suffering.

Why it matters: The ceasefire offers a narrow humanitarian opening even as the broader war continues, while the UN findings add to the documented case for war crimes accountability.

Sources: AP News · NPR World · DW · Al Jazeera · NYT World · AP News · Reuters

Economy & Policy

Oil falls as diplomatic efforts ease Middle East supply fears

Oil prices fell further as renewed diplomatic efforts to end the US-Israeli war with Iran reduced concerns about supply disruption. Separately, senior officials from Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Pakistan and Iran agreed to continue talks aimed at preventing a wider regional war, even as Saudi forces reported intercepting further Houthi missiles and drones. Brent crude fell 5.14% and WTI fell 0.87% in the latest session.

Why it matters: Lower oil prices ease cost pressure on consumers and businesses globally, including transit operators that had faced higher fuel bills from the Iran war.

Sources: CNBC Top News · Al Jazeera · Reuters · France 24 · Bloomberg Markets

UK Chancellor Healey to deliver first budget Wednesday

UK Chancellor John Healey will present his first budget on Wednesday, a key test for Prime Minister Andy Burnham's Labour government as it tries to maintain its position in the polls. Planned measures include shielding low-income pensioners from income tax changes, scrapping a penalty that hit unpaid carers who exceeded earning limits, and cutting tariffs on some food staples. The budget comes as UK shop price inflation rose to its highest level since 2024.

Why it matters: The budget will shape UK household finances and is seen as a test of investor confidence in Burnham's new government.

Sources: BBC Business · Guardian Business · FT World · Bloomberg Politics · BBC Business · Guardian World · Guardian World

Bank of Japan expected to hold rates but signal hawkish shift

The Bank of Japan is expected to keep interest rates unchanged this week, according to a CNBC survey, but all respondents expect it to set the stage for a rate hike in December or January as inflation and wages continue to rise. The yen weakened further, with USD/JPY trading at 157.75.

Why it matters: A hawkish signal from the BOJ could affect global bond and currency markets given Japan's role as a major source of capital flows.

Sources: CNBC Top News · FT World

Business & Tech

Anthropic files for IPO, warning of AI's 'existential risks'

Anthropic filed a Form S-1 for an initial public offering that could value the AI company in the trillions, revealing roughly $80 billion in first-half losses alongside 900% revenue retention. Its roughly 355-page prospectus also warns of what it calls catastrophic and existential risks posed by artificial intelligence. Separately, Wall Street banks and Blackstone are arranging $63 billion to finance Broadcom AI chips for Anthropic. The filing reignited the AI trade, lifting chip, optical and AI-infrastructure stocks on Wall Street and in Asia, with Anthropic set to list on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT.

Why it matters: The IPO is a test of whether AI valuations can be sustained after recent doubts about the AI trade, with ripple effects already visible in chip, semiconductor and optical stocks across US and Asian markets.

Sources: BBC World · FT World · CNBC World · AP News · Bloomberg Politics · Bloomberg Markets · Reuters · BBC Business

Microsoft offers buyouts to 60,000 employees in AI efficiency push

Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to around 60,000 employees as it tries to balance a rapid build-out of AI infrastructure with capital efficiency. The company is seeking to contain costs even as it continues heavy investment in AI data centers and computing capacity.

Why it matters: The move signals that even leading AI investors are tightening costs elsewhere, a sign of the financial strain large-scale AI spending is placing on big tech balance sheets.

Sources: CNBC Top News · Reuters

Meta plans $95 billion stock offering to fund AI expansion

Meta announced plans for a two-part fundraising of $95 billion to cover its AI spending. The announcement came as Elon Musk warned that funding could dry up for hundreds of smaller AI startups facing collapse. Chipmakers including Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom could participate in part of Meta's $35 billion stock-related offering.

Why it matters: The scale of the fundraising underscores how capital-intensive the AI buildout has become, and highlights a widening gap between well-funded giants and smaller AI firms.

Sources: Guardian Business · MarketWatch

DoorDash in talks to buy Instacart for about $17 billion

DoorDash is in advanced talks to acquire grocery delivery rival Instacart in a deal that would value Instacart at roughly $17 billion, according to a source. Analysts say the deal would help DoorDash keep pace with Walmart and Amazon in grocery delivery. DoorDash shares fell 6.1% in the last session.

Why it matters: A combination would consolidate the US grocery and food delivery market, intensifying competition with Walmart and Amazon.

Sources: Reuters · MarketWatch
