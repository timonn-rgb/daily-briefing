Analysis

US stocks rose on Tuesday, with the Dow Jones leading gains at +1.02% to 52,495.88, outperforming the S&P 500 (+0.28% to 7,712.57) and the Nasdaq Composite (-0.02% to 26,424.82). Strong profit reports from Equifax and Hasbro, among other companies, and gains in healthcare stocks drove the advance, while stronger-than-expected tech earnings eased concerns over the AI trade following last week's selloff. The VIX fell 6.13% to 17.31 and the US 10-year yield slipped 2 basis points to 5.12%, even as Goldman Sachs said risk appetite for stocks remains near its weakest level since March.

Asian markets were mixed to higher on Wednesday, with the Nikkei 225 up 0.80% as strong US earnings buoyed sentiment, while the Hang Seng fell 0.35% and the Shanghai Composite fell 0.31%. European benchmarks closed lower on Tuesday, with the DAX down 0.06%, the FTSE 100 down 0.29% and the Euro Stoxx 50 down 0.38%. Oil was mixed, with WTI crude down 2.75% to $98.99 and Brent crude up 1.35% to $110.26 amid ongoing attacks near the Strait of Hormuz.

US futures point to a higher open, with S&P 500 futures up 0.29% and Nasdaq 100 futures up 0.74%. In Europe/Berlin time, UK CPI data for September is due at 08:00, with inflation forecast to rise to 3.4% from 3.1%. Meta and Tesla report quarterly earnings today.

World news

Geopolitics

US envoy to meet Putin as Trump presses Ukraine on peace deal

US envoy Steve Witkoff is set to meet Russian President Vladimir Putin as President Trump presses Ukraine to reach a peace settlement, saying "I don't care how it's done." Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said the US has resumed sharing intelligence on Russian missile strikes after an apparent suspension last week, and that an early-warning system now "works" following a call with Secretary of State Marco Rubio. Trump also said he asked Putin to release American detainee Stephen Hubbard. Relations between Trump and Zelensky remain tense despite what Ukrainian officials described as "constructive" talks in Oslo with Witkoff and Jared Kushner.

Why it matters: The outcome of US mediation and the status of intelligence-sharing directly affect Ukraine's ability to defend against Russian strikes and the prospects for ending the war.

Sources: BBC World · Reuters · Al Jazeera · AP News · BBC World · Guardian World · Reuters

Protests and troop deployments spread across Europe

Italy's government approved deploying 500 additional soldiers to Milan and tougher security measures, including harsher penalties for violent protesters, after weekend riots left more than 100 police officers injured and shops looted. France braced for fresh protests this weekend, with Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu promising a "massive" police presence after riots left more than 3,000 businesses damaged, including a historic town hall set alight in Limoges. In Portugal, cost-of-living protesters returned to camps in Lisbon and Porto after police cleared them and arrested 55 people. Across the continent, protesters have cited high living costs, housing shortages and strained public services.

Why it matters: Sustained unrest across multiple European countries over living costs and housing could pressure governments into policy responses and add political risk in markets watching the region.

Sources: CNBC Top News · France 24 · Al Jazeera · CNBC Top News · Al Jazeera · France 24

Netanyahu pushes to resume strikes on Iran

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is pushing to resume strikes against Iran, having made plans for an extensive operation earlier this month that were held off after Donald Trump ruled out an attack before the US midterm elections. The US said reaching a peace deal with Tehran remains "extremely difficult" while attacks near the Strait of Hormuz continue, and Iran said Saudi Arabia and the Houthis have not responded to a mediation offer. Netanyahu faces elections of his own next week.

Why it matters: A resumption of Israeli strikes on Iran could disrupt shipping through the Strait of Hormuz and add to volatility in oil markets.

Sources: FT World · FT World · Al Jazeera · Reuters · France 24

Drone strike on Sudan-South Sudan border market kills over 150

A drone strike on a market in Abyei, a disputed region on the Sudan-South Sudan border, killed more than 150 people.

Why it matters: The strike underscores the continuing violence in the contested Abyei region, which both Sudan and South Sudan claim.

Sources: Al Jazeera · DW · AP News

Economy & Policy

Food importer sues Trump over Canada tariffs

A New Jersey food importer filed a lawsuit challenging President Trump's 50% tariffs on Canadian goods, arguing the White House exceeded its authority and bypassed Congress by using a Depression-era law, causing "immediate harm to American businesses." It is the latest in a series of legal challenges to the administration's tariff policy.

Why it matters: A successful challenge could force changes to tariffs that affect the cost of Canadian imports for US businesses.

Sources: BBC World · CNBC Top News · AP News · Al Jazeera · Reuters

Business & Tech

Musk confirms SpaceX-Tesla merger talks

Elon Musk said SpaceX and Tesla have each formed special committees to study a possible merger, calling the tie-up a "good idea" with "a lot of merit" given the companies' complementary work on AI. SpaceX reported a profit and issued upbeat guidance, with revenue more than doubling on growth in its Starlink and AI businesses. Musk also said the companies' joint chip venture, Terafab, is targeting 2028 for volume production. News of the potential deal, which could form a company worth $3.5 trillion, lifted Tesla's stock.

Why it matters: A combination of this scale would reshape two of the most closely watched companies in markets and tie Tesla's fortunes more closely to SpaceX's AI and satellite businesses.

Sources: BBC World · Al Jazeera · Al Jazeera · MarketWatch · Reuters · NYT Business · Reuters

AI data center boom faces growing investor scrutiny

Shares in Australian data center operator NextDC plunged 40% after it cut its earnings forecast, citing power supply delays at its Melbourne facility amid local opposition. South Korean AI computing supplier Elice swung sharply, recovering much of a 32% trading-debut slump after upbeat US tech earnings lifted sentiment, while Bloomberg reported that data center firm DayOne is weighing delaying or shrinking its US IPO amid data-center volatility. Separately, data centers' power and water use has become a bipartisan issue ahead of the US midterm elections.

Why it matters: Swings in data center stocks and IPO plans signal investors are becoming more selective about which AI infrastructure bets can generate durable profits.

Sources: CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Markets · France 24

Netflix shares fall despite earnings beat

Netflix reported third-quarter revenue up 12% and beat Wall Street's earnings expectations, but shares fell after the company forecast a fourth consecutive quarter of slowing sales growth and flagged weaker advertising revenue guidance. Co-CEO Ted Sarandos acknowledged "some investor concern about our view hours growth" and said "we could do better," citing intense streaming competition and stagnant viewership, particularly in the US and Canada.

Why it matters: Slowing subscriber engagement at the dominant streaming platform raises questions about growth across the wider streaming industry.

Sources: CNBC Top News · FT World · MarketWatch · Reuters · NYT Business

Ambani launches India's biggest-ever IPO

Indian billionaire Mukesh Ambani launched Jio Platforms' initial public offering, which opened for public bids in a record $3.2 billion sale and could value the low-cost broadband provider at up to £81 billion. The offering has already drawn $854 million from anchor investors including BlackRock, Fidelity and Capital Group. It is India's biggest-ever IPO and comes despite a broader downturn in the country's stock market.

Why it matters: The IPO's reception will test investor appetite for Indian equities at a time when the broader market is under pressure.

Sources: Guardian World · Bloomberg Markets · Reuters

Other

Ebola outbreak reaches Nigeria as Kenya cases rise

Nigeria confirmed its first case of the current Ebola outbreak, in a woman in the capital Abuja who had traveled from Uganda, while Kenya's case count reached nine. The outbreak began in the Democratic Republic of Congo in mid-May and has now spread into Western Africa. Africa CDC chief Jean Kaseya described it as an "extremely serious threat" and urged greater international support, and the US has advised its citizens to reconsider travel to Kenya.

Why it matters: The outbreak's spread into Nigeria, Africa's most populous country, raises the risk of wider regional transmission.

Sources: Reuters · AP News · Reuters · AP News · Guardian World · BBC World · NPR World
