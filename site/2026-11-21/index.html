Analysis

US stocks sold off sharply on Friday, with the S&P 500 falling 3.70% to 6,910.39 and the Dow Jones down 1.93% to 48,147.33, both closing in correction territory roughly 10% below their records. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.28% to 22,617.73, erasing its entire 2026 gain, as Anthropic's stock tumbled on its second day of trading and Nvidia fell further, capping its longest weekly losing streak since 2008. The VIX jumped 26.20% to 35.89 and the 10-year Treasury yield fell 14 basis points to 4.91% as investors sought safer assets. Chip stocks as a group lost roughly $2.3 trillion in value over the week.

Asian markets followed Wall Street lower: the Nikkei 225 fell 5.06% to 56,436.65, the Hang Seng dropped 1.89%, and the Shanghai Composite lost 1.22%. In Europe, the DAX fell 2.60% to 24,802.78, the Euro Stoxx 50 dropped 2.81%, and the FTSE 100 declined 1.02%, with US futures pointing to further weakness at the open (S&P 500 futures -3.64%, Nasdaq 100 futures -5.54%).

No economic data releases or earnings from the watch list are scheduled in Europe/Berlin time today. Markets remain focused on the fallout from Nvidia's reported SEC accounting probe, Trump's threat of penalties on US firms building AI data centers abroad, and the ongoing COP31 climate talks, which have run into overtime without agreement on a fossil-fuel phaseout.

World news

Geopolitics

Iran war widens: tanker incidents near Hormuz, Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia

The UK's maritime agency reported two tanker incidents near Oman, and Iran said a vessel was hit by a mine east of the Strait of Hormuz; Iran also said it downed US drones over the strait. Oil shipments through Hormuz have fallen as Iranian strikes increase, with one analyst calling the recovery in Middle Eastern crude exports "increasingly fragile." Iran-backed Houthi rebels launched fresh strikes on Saudi Arabia, and the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted three ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh. In Iraq, army and police units entered territory controlled by the Iran-backed militia Kataib Hezbollah for the first time in over a decade.

Why it matters: Continued attacks around the Strait of Hormuz, a critical oil transit chokepoint, and the widening of Iran-linked conflicts across the region raise risks to global energy supplies and regional stability.

Sources: Reuters · CNBC Top News · FT World · France 24 · Al Jazeera · NYT World · Reuters

Russia accused of Poland airspace violation, Denmark assassination plot, Baltic ship attack

Three Russian projectiles, including a suspected jet-powered drone and a missile, came down in Poland near the Ukrainian border, which NATO called a grave violation of Polish airspace. Denmark accused Russian intelligence services of plotting to assassinate a US-Ukrainian rapper in Copenhagen, expelled a Russian defense attaché, and the EU's Ursula von der Leyen condemned the alleged plot. Separately, Russia said a cargo ship bound for its Kaliningrad exclave was struck by drones and set on fire in neutral waters in the Gulf of Finland. Denmark is also pushing the EU to tighten travel restrictions on Russians after a separate foiled plot.

Why it matters: A cluster of incidents involving Russia — airspace violations, an alleged assassination plot on EU soil, and an attack on shipping in the Baltic — point to rising friction between Russia and NATO members well beyond Ukraine's borders.

Sources: Al Jazeera · France 24 · AP News · France 24 · BBC World · France 24 · NYT World · Reuters · Bloomberg Markets · Reuters

EU and UK strike post-Brexit 'reset' deal on trade and youth mobility

The UK and the EU signed agreements at a Brussels summit aimed at reducing red tape for food exports, linking emissions trading schemes, and allowing young people to live and work more freely between the two. UK foreign secretary Ed Miliband said rejoining the EU remains "on the table" in future informal talks, while the prime minister called it "a good day for Britain and Europe." Miliband said the deal is not dependent on a separate agreement over tuition fees for EU students in Britain.

Why it matters: The agreements mark the most significant effort yet to rebuild UK-EU relations since Brexit, with potential implications for trade costs and labor mobility between the two economies.

Sources: Guardian World · DW · France 24 · France 24 · BBC World

UN talks agree to keep discussing 'killer robots' but set no binding ban

States at UN talks on lethal autonomous weapons agreed to continue discussions aiming to regulate such "killer robots" by 2028, extending negotiations for two years, but reached no legally binding rules or firm commitment to rein them in. The talks took place under the UN's Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons.

Why it matters: The absence of binding limits on autonomous weapons leaves a regulatory gap as militaries increasingly integrate AI into weapons systems.

Sources: Reuters · CNBC Top News · AP News · NPR World · Al Jazeera · DW

Economy & Policy

Trump threatens penalties on US companies building AI data centers abroad

President Trump said on Friday he would impose "tremendous penalties" on American technology companies that build data centers outside the United States, though he gave no further details and said members of his own team were hearing about the policy for the first time. The threat came during a wide-ranging White House press conference in which Trump also said he has "fantastic relationships" with Putin and Xi. The announcement adds a new layer of uncertainty for AI companies already facing a sharp sell-off in their shares.

Why it matters: An unplanned threat to penalize offshore data-center investment could disrupt AI companies' global expansion plans and adds policy uncertainty just as AI-related stocks are under heavy selling pressure.

Sources: Bloomberg Politics · CNBC Top News · Guardian World · BBC World · AP News

COP31 climate talks run into overtime as fossil-fuel phaseout remains unresolved

COP31 negotiations extended into overtime after nearly two weeks, with countries still divided on how to address climate change; a new draft declaration does not mention phasing out fossil fuels. China pledged to pursue rapid low-carbon electrification of its economy and said it would help other countries do the same, with Cop31 delegates being urged to commit to deriving 35% of energy from electricity by 2035.

Why it matters: A deadlock on fossil-fuel phaseout language at a major UN climate summit signals continued difficulty translating global climate commitments into binding action, with implications for energy policy and investment worldwide.

Sources: DW · BBC World · AP News · Guardian World

Business & Tech

Wall Street's AI-driven sell-off pushes S&P 500 and Dow into correction

On Friday the S&P 500 fell 3.70% and the Dow Jones fell 1.93%, both closing in correction territory, down 10% from their records, while the Nasdaq Composite dropped 5.28% and erased its entire 2026 gain. The rout deepened after Anthropic's stock tumbled on its second day of trading following its IPO, with investors questioning the scale of AI infrastructure spending. Nvidia shares fell further, capping their longest weekly losing streak since the 2008 financial crisis, as chip stocks lost $2.3 trillion in value over the week. The VIX volatility index jumped 26.2% to 35.89 and the US 10-year yield fell 14 basis points to 4.91% as investors sought safety.

Why it matters: A correction across the three main US indices, led by the collapse of AI-related stocks, signals investors are reassessing the sustainability of the AI investment boom, with potential spillover to global equity and bond markets.

Sources: BBC World · FT World · Guardian World · CNBC Top News · CNBC World · AP News · CNBC Top News · MarketWatch

Nvidia faces SEC accounting probe amid espionage allegations tied to China

Nvidia is reportedly facing a Securities and Exchange Commission investigation into its accounting practices, following questions about its chip sales in Asia. Separately, Alice Huang, a woman at the center of an espionage scandal involving Nvidia, has been linked to a network of Chinese tech firms and defense contractors with ties to China's military. The news adds to pressure on Nvidia, whose shares have fallen sharply amid the broader AI sell-off following the exit of CEO Jensen Huang.

Why it matters: Regulatory scrutiny and espionage allegations compound uncertainty at one of the most important companies in the global AI supply chain, at a moment when Nvidia stock is already under heavy pressure.

Sources: NYT Business · FT World · NYT Business · Bloomberg Markets · CNBC Top News

Missouri judge approves Bayer's $7.25 billion Roundup settlement

A Missouri judge approved Bayer's $7.25 billion settlement of lawsuits alleging its Roundup weedkiller causes cancer, a deal that faced claims from roughly 65,000 people who said they developed non-Hodgkin lymphoma after using the product. The ruling is expected to resolve tens of thousands of related lawsuits in a nationwide class action.

Why it matters: The settlement removes a major legal overhang for Bayer, one of the world's largest chemical and pharmaceutical companies, after years of litigation costs.

Sources: Reuters · DW · NYT Business · Guardian Business · AP News · FT World

Grand Theft Auto VI hits $3.8 billion in first-day sales, a record for entertainment

Rockstar Games said Grand Theft Auto VI generated $3.79 billion in retail sales on its first day of release, with publisher Take-Two Interactive confirming the figure. The launch is being called the biggest entertainment debut in history, surpassing the box-office run of the highest-grossing film ever. Take-Two said the game set a sales record for the industry.

Why it matters: The launch underscores the video game industry's growing weight in global entertainment spending and is a significant revenue event for Take-Two Interactive.

Sources: Guardian World · CNBC Top News · AP News · BBC Business
