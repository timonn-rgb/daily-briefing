Analysis

Wall Street had its worst session since June on Friday as anxiety over ballooning AI spending intensified. The S&P 500 fell 1.82% to 7,580.07 and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.86% to 25,437.00, its lowest close in more than two months, while the VIX volatility index jumped 12.91% to 19.42. Intel had its worst day since the 1970s after weak results, OpenAI cut its funding target by $10bn, and S&P Global revised Meta's outlook to negative on capital-spending and cash-flow concerns. The 10-year Treasury yield eased 6 basis points to 4.93%.

Asian markets were mixed: the Nikkei 225 fell 2.12% tracking the US tech rout and the Shanghai Composite lost 1.44%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 1.46%. In Europe, the DAX slipped 0.85% and the Euro Stoxx 50 fell 1.22%, while London's FTSE 100 edged up 0.07%. Oil rose on renewed Iran-linked attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, with Brent crude up 2.40% to $117.64 and WTI up 2.08% to $104.12. Gold fell 3.29% to its lowest in a year, and bitcoin was little changed at $77,158.50 despite the record Binance hack.

No economic data or watch-list earnings are scheduled for today. Markets will track further fallout from the Binance hack and the AI-spending selloff, plus comments from Federal Reserve officials after Presidents Alberto Musalem and Lorie Logan both signalled the Fed may need to resume raising rates. US midterm elections on Tuesday, with early voting already above the 2022 record, are also in focus.

World news

Geopolitics

Mine attacks set four tankers ablaze in Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said four tankers caught fire after being struck by mines in the Strait of Hormuz, including one carrying 27 Indian crew members. It was reported as the first known successful use of such mines in the waterway since the US-Iran war began. Tanker operators are continuing to transit the strait despite the escalation, drawn by sharply higher freight rates. Trump said the conflict with Iran would be 'wrapped up soon.'

Why it matters: The Strait of Hormuz carries a large share of global seaborne oil trade, and further attacks there could escalate the war and keep pushing up oil and freight prices.

Sources: CNBC Top News · DW · Al Jazeera · Al Jazeera · AP News · Reuters · Reuters · Reuters

US approves $2.1bn Patriot missile sale to Ukraine

The US approved a proposed $2.1bn sale of 500 Patriot PAC-3 interceptor missiles to Ukraine, one of the most significant steps in supplying defensive weapons to Kyiv since Trump took office in 2025. Ukrainian President Zelenskyy welcomed the decision but warned delivery could take years. Russia's foreign minister Sergey Lavrov called Zelenskyy's comments unhelpful and said Russia's position was unchanged.

Why it matters: Patriot interceptors are described as the only effective weapon against Russian ballistic missiles, so the sale could affect Ukraine's ability to defend its cities, though the long delivery timeline limits any near-term impact.

Sources: Al Jazeera · FT World · Guardian World · BBC World · AP News · Reuters · Bloomberg Politics

Riot police deployed as Poland's top court crisis escalates

Polish riot police faced off with protesters outside the Constitutional Tribunal in Warsaw after the government suspended the court's chief judge, Bogdan Swieczkowski, as part of a long-running dispute over the court's legitimacy. President Karol Nawrocki cut short a trip to Senegal to join the protests, while Prime Minister Donald Tusk defended the move as necessary to 'restore the rule of law.' The escalation follows the suspension of two senior justices and moves toward criminal charges against them.

Why it matters: The standoff deepens a rule-of-law dispute between Poland's president and government ahead of a parliamentary election next year, and highlights ongoing tensions over judicial independence within the EU.

Sources: Guardian World · Al Jazeera · BBC World · FT World · DW · Al Jazeera · Bloomberg Politics · AP News · Reuters

Canada and EU unveil new alliance as old order unravels

Canada and the European Union announced the 'Alliance for the Future' at a summit in Montreal, describing it as open to other like-minded countries in a world 'shaken by new storms.' Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney said Canada is seeking an 'associate membership' with the EU to diversify ties beyond the US, despite some German pushback on the term. The move is framed as part of Canada's effort to reduce American influence over its economy and foreign policy.

Why it matters: The initiative signals a broader realignment among traditional US allies as confidence in the transatlantic relationship under Trump weakens, with potential implications for trade and diplomatic ties involving the EU.

Sources: Guardian World · Bloomberg Politics · NYT World · DW

No clear winner in Israel election

Final results from Israel's election showed no outright winner, with Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's party and allies taking 53 seats, short of the majority needed to govern. The main opposition bloc, led by Gadi Eisenkot, won 48 seats. Commentary noted Netanyahu has lost his majority but remains difficult to defeat politically.

Why it matters: A prolonged coalition-formation process in Israel could add uncertainty to the country's political direction amid ongoing regional conflicts.

Sources: NYT World · France 24 · FT World

Economy & Policy

US midterm elections loom over jittery investors

With US midterm elections on Tuesday, early voting has already surpassed the 2022 record, with more than 48.5 million ballots cast. Investors are weighing whether the vote could shift control of Congress, though some commentary notes inflation, interest rates, AI spending and the Iran war are seen as more important market drivers than the election itself. Wall Street is also bracing for a busy week of earnings, including Apple and AMD, alongside the monthly jobs report.

Why it matters: A change in control of Congress could affect the direction of fiscal and regulatory policy for the rest of Trump's term, though markets are described as more focused on AI spending, rates and the Iran war.

Sources: FT World · FT World · AP News · Bloomberg Politics · CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News

Fed officials signal possible return to rate hikes

Federal Reserve Presidents Alberto Musalem and Lorie Logan both indicated the Fed may need to resume raising interest rates, offering early insight into the central bank's next moves. Separate commentary noted that more interest-rate hikes are seen as needed to fight inflation.

Why it matters: A pivot back toward rate hikes would mark a reversal for markets that had been pricing in a less restrictive Fed, and could pressure both bond and equity prices.

Sources: CNBC Top News · MarketWatch

Business & Tech

AI spending fears trigger worst tech selloff since June

US tech stocks suffered their sharpest drop since June as investors questioned whether massive artificial-intelligence spending will pay off. Intel shares had their worst day since the 1970s on weak results, wiping about $12bn off the US government's stake in the company, and the broader chip sector followed it lower. OpenAI cut its funding target by $10bn to about $20bn, and S&P Global revised Meta's outlook to negative over its capex and cash-flow trajectory. Reports also surfaced that Anthropic could receive a junk rating after its planned IPO, adding to concerns about the industry's borrowing spree.

Why it matters: The selloff signals growing investor skepticism about the AI capital-spending boom that has powered the stock market's gains, and any further unwinding could ripple through broader indices and credit markets.

Sources: FT World · FT World · BBC World · CNBC Top News · NYT Business · Reuters · MarketWatch · Reuters · Reuters · NYT Business · Guardian Business · FT Markets

North Korea-linked hackers steal record $6.3bn from Binance

Hackers believed to be linked to North Korea stole about $6.3bn from crypto exchange Binance, in what is considered the largest crypto theft on record. Binance said an engineer's laptop was compromised, that roughly $3bn of the funds has been frozen, and that it will cover customer losses. Investigators described a 'cat and mouse' race to recover the funds before they can be laundered. President Trump said the US government was looking into whether North Korea was responsible.

Why it matters: The theft underscores persistent security vulnerabilities at crypto exchanges and highlights how crypto theft has become a major source of foreign currency for North Korea's isolated economy.

Sources: BBC World · FT Markets · CNBC Top News · BBC World · CNBC Top News · BBC World · AP News · FT Markets · FT Markets · Bloomberg Markets · NYT Business · NYT Business · Bloomberg Politics · NYT Business · Reuters · NYT Business · CNBC Top News

BP, Chevron and Exxon post bumper profits on war-driven refining margins

BP's quarterly profit hit an 18-year high of $8.46bn, which climate campaigners criticized as 'obscene,' as the Middle East war pushed up oil and gas prices. Chevron and Exxon hit all-time highs as refining margins surged, with global diesel prices climbing after Ukraine and Iran struck refineries in Russia and the Middle East. Exxon and Chevron's profits beat analyst forecasts.

Why it matters: The results show how the Ukraine and Iran conflicts are redistributing profits toward oil majors through higher refining margins, even as higher fuel costs weigh on consumers and other businesses.

Sources: Guardian World · CNBC Top News · Reuters
