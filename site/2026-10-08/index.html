Analysis

Wall Street retreated from record highs on Wednesday as Treasury yields climbed toward a 24-year high before easing after a solid 10-year note auction, and Fed minutes showed all 19 officials backing September's rate hike, with some policymakers signaling support for another increase before year-end. The S&P 500 fell 0.22%, the Nasdaq slipped 0.22% and the Dow dropped 0.66%; the 10-year yield ended at 5.28%, while a Fed survey showed the one-year inflation outlook at its highest since May 2023.

Asian markets traded lower Thursday, with the Nikkei down 0.86%, the Hang Seng down 0.69% and the Shanghai Composite down 0.27%, even as Samsung forecast a record $80 billion quarterly profit on AI chip demand; Japanese equities were also pressured by TOPIX weighting changes and a wave of AI-related bond issuance. European benchmarks closed sharply lower Wednesday, with the DAX down 1.35%, the Euro Stoxx 50 down 1.47% and the FTSE 100 down 0.79%. US futures were little changed overnight.

Oil extended its advance, with WTI up 1.81% and Brent up 2.13%, on supply concerns after Houthi missile strikes on Saudi airports and reports that Washington is weighing renewed military action against Iran; gold rose 0.44% while bitcoin fell 3.42%. On today's calendar, Bank of England Governor Bailey speaks at 14:15 Berlin time in a high-impact event, and no earnings are due from the watch list.

World news

Geopolitics

Middle East tensions escalate as Houthi strikes hit Saudi airports

Houthi forces fired missiles that struck Saudi Arabia's Riyadh and Abha airports, killing three people and wounding dozens, marking an escalation in the group's campaign against the Saudi-led coalition. A tanker was also hit by projectiles off Qatar's coast, and a supertanker charter from the Gulf Coast to China reportedly now costs ten times pre-war rates. Separately, NBC News reported that President Trump and his national security team have discussed resuming large-scale US military operations against Iran in the coming weeks. Oil prices rose further on the supply concerns, with Brent crude climbing above $102 a barrel.

Why it matters: A wider regional conflict could further disrupt Gulf shipping and oil supply, adding to inflation pressure already weighing on bond markets and stocks.

France braces for more mass student protests

French students called for fresh protests on Thursday, two days after a large turnout demanding more education funding, as the government offered conciliatory gestures to try to defuse the crisis. More than 6,100 people have been arrested since the protests began, most of them minors with no prior record, and France has suspended police use of stun grenades after a student lost a hand. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu has sought to calm both protesters and jittery bond markets, saying the education ministry avoided cuts in the latest budget while insisting public spending must still be reduced.

Why it matters: Prolonged unrest adds political risk to France's efforts to pass a budget and stabilize bond markets already rattled by fiscal concerns.

Sources: NYT World · NYT World · BBC World · France 24 · NYT World · France 24 · France 24 · France 24

Bolsonaro's son leads Brazil race, sparking mass protests

Flavio Bolsonaro advanced to the front of Brazil's presidential race after placing first in Sunday's vote, prompting thousands of students and other protesters to march in Rio de Janeiro against a potential far-right return to power. Bolsonaro has pledged a broad review of taxes introduced under President Lula. Climate campaigners say a Bolsonaro win could threaten gains made in curbing Amazon deforestation. Brazil's large bloc of undecided and abstaining voters may ultimately decide the October 25 runoff.

Why it matters: As a major emerging-market economy and steward of the Amazon, the election's outcome carries implications for global climate policy and Latin American markets.

Sources: FT World · DW · Al Jazeera · France 24 · AP News · Reuters · AP News · Guardian World

Economy & Policy

Fed minutes show hawkish unity as yields pressure stocks

Minutes from the Federal Reserve's September meeting showed all 19 officials backed that month's rate hike, and some policymakers signaled support for a further increase before year-end, though the timing remains unclear. A Fed survey found the one-year inflation outlook rose to 3.9%, the highest since May 2023. The 10-year Treasury yield touched a 24-year high before easing slightly after a solid bond auction. Wall Street pulled back from record highs as a result, with the S&P 500 down 0.22% and the Dow down 0.66%.

Why it matters: Expectations of further rate hikes and persistent inflation concerns are pushing borrowing costs higher, squeezing rate-sensitive sectors of the market.

Sources: CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · CNBC World · Reuters · MarketWatch · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Politics · Reuters · Reuters · MarketWatch · Bloomberg Politics · AP News · Bloomberg Politics · MarketWatch

EU and China open tense trade talks in Beijing

The EU's trade chief, Maroš Šefčovič, traveled to Beijing for two days of negotiations aimed at avoiding a damaging trade war, with talks focused on a roughly €1 billion daily EU-China trade deficit and Chinese rare-earth export restrictions. Brussels is weighing tougher trade measures, blaming a surge in Chinese exports for the loss of tens of thousands of European manufacturing jobs. The EU is reportedly taking a harder line as surging Chinese exports pressure key European industries, including the car market.

Why it matters: The outcome could reshape EU-China trade flows and tariffs, with direct consequences for European manufacturers and global supply chains.

Sources: NYT World · FT World · FT World · FT World · Bloomberg Markets · Al Jazeera

India's rupee nears record low as RBI hikes rates amid protests

The Indian rupee neared a record low even after the Reserve Bank of India signaled further monetary tightening, with a weak monsoon raising food-price risks and making imports more expensive. Indian shares opened lower, pressured by the hawkish rate move and rising oil prices. The market moves came as opposition protests grew over the removal of 130 million people from voter lists, with Congress leader Rahul Gandhi briefly detained.

Why it matters: Currency weakness combined with political unrest adds pressure on India's central bank as it tries to balance inflation control with growth support.

Sources: DW · AP News · Bloomberg Markets · Reuters

Business & Tech

Samsung forecasts record $80 billion profit on AI chip boom

Samsung Electronics said preliminary third-quarter operating profit would top 100 trillion won (about $80 billion) for the first time, a roughly ninefold increase, as demand for AI memory chips surged. The world's largest memory-chip maker credited the results to AI-driven chip demand and said its latest folding devices should provide further support. Some investors questioned whether the pace of AI-related spending driving such profits is sustainable. Samsung is one of several Asian chipmakers benefiting from the broader AI buildout.

Why it matters: The results underline how concentrated AI-related capital spending has become a dominant driver of corporate earnings and market sentiment in Asia.

Sources: CNBC Top News · FT World · Bloomberg Markets · BBC Business · Reuters

Apollo, banks in talks to finance SpaceX's $40bn Nvidia GPU buy

Apollo Global Management and other lenders are discussing financing for SpaceX's roughly $40 billion purchase of Nvidia GPUs, as the space and satellite company expands its AI computing capacity. The talks come as yields on SpaceX bonds and other AI-related debt have risen since the company's record IPO in June. Analysts pointed to the GPU purchase and a recent Goldman Sachs price-target increase on Nvidia as signs SpaceX's AI ambitions are materializing.

Why it matters: Large debt-financed AI infrastructure deals are adding to a broader wave of AI-related borrowing that is being watched for signs of stress in credit markets.

Sources: CNBC Top News · CNBC World · CNBC World · CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News

Webull stock drops 18% on China ties national security findings

Shares of trading platform Webull fell 18% after a congressional committee found the company is not the ordinary US firm it claims to be, saying it exposes customer data to Chinese surveillance risk. The panel's report challenges Webull's characterization of its ownership and data practices. The finding adds Webull to a list of China-linked companies facing scrutiny from US lawmakers.

Why it matters: The findings could lead to further regulatory action against Webull and intensify broader US scrutiny of Chinese-linked financial technology firms.

Sources: CNBC Top News · Reuters

Other

WHO presses Russia for details on suspected plague case

The World Health Organization said it still lacks a "full picture" from Russia following the suspected death of a 28-year-old laboratory worker from pneumonic plague in the Irkutsk region, and urged Moscow to share more information. Nearly 200 people were placed under medical observation this week. Russian authorities have dismissed reports of a second case as false and said the worker's death does not risk an epidemic, while President Trump said he does not believe the case is linked to a bioweapon and plans to discuss it with President Putin.

Why it matters: Limited transparency from Russia complicates international health authorities' ability to assess and respond to any wider outbreak risk.
