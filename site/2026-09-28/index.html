Analysis

The S&P 500 rose 0.51% to 7,743.41 on Friday, with the Nasdaq Composite up 0.48% and the Dow Jones up 0.93%, while the US 10-year Treasury yield ticked up 2 basis points to 5.18%.

In Europe, the DAX was flat, the FTSE 100 rose 0.27% and the Euro Stoxx 50 added 0.07%. Asian markets were mixed, with the Nikkei 225 down 0.73%, the Hang Seng up 0.54% and the Shanghai Composite down 1.67%. US futures pointed lower — S&P 500 futures fell 0.44% and Nasdaq 100 futures fell 0.79% — and the VIX jumped 9.41% to 16.27, after President Trump rejected an Iranian proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. WTI crude rose 3.48% to $95.63, gold fell 3.18% to $4,183.90, and the dollar index rose 0.15% to 101.12.

No economic data releases or watch-list earnings are scheduled for Monday. Investors are instead focused on developments in the Iran conflict, the UK's investigation into a possible bomb plot at RAF Fairford, and Nvidia's $150 billion buyback announcement.

World news

Geopolitics

Trump rejects Iran's Hormuz proposal, sending oil and bond yields higher

President Trump rejected an Iranian proposal aimed at reopening the Strait of Hormuz and ending the conflict, calling it unacceptable. Iran's foreign ministry said it has no plans to negotiate with the US despite earlier signals talks could resume. Oil prices jumped in response, with WTI crude up 3.48% to $95.63, while headlines described Brent crude climbing above $107 a barrel. The impasse also pushed government bond yields higher, with reports describing the US 10-year yield rising above 5.2% intraday.

Why it matters: A prolonged Hormuz standoff threatens to keep oil and shipping costs elevated and adds to inflation and borrowing-cost pressure just as central banks weigh their next moves.

Sources: CNBC Top News · FT World · Al Jazeera · NYT Business · Guardian World · CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Markets

UK investigates possible Iran link to bomb plot at US-used air base

British counter-terrorism police arrested five UK nationals, all men in their twenties from London, near RAF Fairford, an air base used by US forces striking Iran. Police are investigating whether the men, held on suspicion of preparing a terrorist act, have a link to Iran. UK Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said there is "no doubt" Iran poses a threat to Britain, though he declined to directly tie the arrests to the war. Officials said UK security services had been monitoring threats to the base and had tightened security beforehand.

Why it matters: An attack on a base used for US strikes against Iran would mark a significant escalation of the conflict onto British soil and could harden Western policy toward Tehran.

Sources: FT World · CNBC Top News · Guardian World · NYT World · Bloomberg Politics · Bloomberg Politics · Guardian World · FT World

France's far-right National Rally wins record Senate seats

Marine Le Pen's National Rally and its allies won a record 14 seats in France's partial Senate elections, effectively quadrupling their presence in the 348-seat chamber. Le Pen called the result a "victory" that will boost her 2027 presidential campaign. A separate poll showed Le Pen and hard-left leader Jean-Luc Mélenchon reaching the presidential run-off. The Senate remains dominated by right-wing parties overall.

Why it matters: The gains strengthen the far right's institutional foothold in France months before a presidential election that could reshape the country's, and the EU's, political direction.

Sources: France 24 · Guardian World · Reuters · Al Jazeera

Serbia's President Vucic resigns to run for prime minister

Serbia's populist President Aleksandar Vucic resigned in order to run for prime minister at an early parliamentary election. The snap vote follows months of student-led protests against his government. Analysts say the early election is unlikely to end Serbia's political crisis or resolve the pressure Vucic has faced.

Why it matters: The move is an unusual attempt by an entrenched leader to hold onto power through a different office, and its outcome will shape Serbia's political stability and its path toward EU accession.

Sources: AP News · Guardian World · Al Jazeera · France 24 · Al Jazeera

Economy & Policy

US and China detail deal to cut tariffs on $60 billion of goods

The US and China published lists covering tariff cuts on about $60 billion of goods, including US imports of toys, sports equipment and Christmas decorations, and Chinese purchases of American farm products. Soybeans and LNG were notably left off the list of goods receiving relief. The move follows last week's Trump-Xi summit in Washington, where the two leaders signaled plans to meet twice more this year. Analysts said "tangible outcomes" are still needed for the truce to hold.

Why it matters: The partial tariff rollback eases some trade tension between the world's two largest economies but leaves major agricultural and energy sticking points unresolved.

Sources: CNBC Top News · NYT Business · AP News · Reuters · Bloomberg Markets · CNBC Top News

UK diesel prices hit record high amid Iran-driven supply crunch

The average price of diesel at UK pumps reached an all-time high of 199.18p a litre, according to the RAC motoring body, as the conflict involving Iran continues to disrupt fuel supply. Trump is reportedly considering a US diesel export ban to address a global supply crunch, though analysts warned it could push up global diesel prices and raise US gasoline costs. Goldman Sachs estimated such a ban could add $0.30 per gallon to US retail gasoline prices.

Why it matters: Sustained high diesel costs raise expenses for hauliers, farmers and industry across Europe, adding to inflation pressure while central banks are already contending with higher energy prices.

Sources: BBC Business · Guardian Business · CNBC Top News · MarketWatch · DW

UK housebuilder shares surge on new 'Your First Home' scheme

Shares in UK housebuilders rallied after Prime Minister Andy Burnham unveiled a "Your First Home" plan to help first-time buyers onto the property ladder. Analysts named specific housebuilders as likely beneficiaries of the new government program. The announcement came as European stocks edged higher despite rising bond yields. The scheme adds to a series of policy announcements at the Labour Party conference in Liverpool this week.

Why it matters: Government support for first-time buyers could boost housebuilding activity and provide a rare bright spot for UK consumer-facing stocks amid broader market pressure from rising yields and oil prices.

Sources: CNBC World · Reuters · FT World · Bloomberg Markets

Business & Tech

Nvidia announces $150bn buyback and new AI agent safety platform

Nvidia unveiled a $150 billion share buyback program, one of the largest ever announced, to run through January 2028, which the company said reflects confidence in its long-term growth. Separately, Nvidia released a software platform designed to stop AI agents from misbehaving, saying it could have prevented an incident involving OpenAI and Hugging Face. The buyback comes as gains in Nvidia's share price have slowed this year.

Why it matters: The buyback signals Nvidia's confidence despite a slowdown in share gains, while the safety platform responds to growing concern about AI agents operating with less oversight.

Sources: FT World · MarketWatch · CNBC Top News · Reuters · AP News

Australia's Northern Star rejects $27bn takeover bid from Gold Fields

Northern Star Resources, Australia's largest gold miner, rejected a $27 billion takeover proposal from South Africa's Gold Fields. Elliott Management called on Northern Star to hold talks with Gold Fields regardless. Northern Star's shares rose after the rejection was announced. A combination would have created the world's second-largest gold miner.

Why it matters: The rejected bid highlights continued consolidation interest in the gold mining sector as elevated gold prices draw takeover activity.

Sources: FT World · CNBC World · Reuters

Other

Mass shootings in South Africa leave 28 dead

Two mass shootings hours apart in South Africa left at least 28 people dead, according to police, who are hunting the gunmen. The attacks come amid persistently high levels of gang-related armed violence in the country. Authorities have not yet named suspects or a motive.

Why it matters: The killings underscore South Africa's ongoing struggle with armed violence ahead of municipal elections on November 4, when local policing is expected to be a central issue.

Sources: AP News · Guardian World · BBC World · Reuters
