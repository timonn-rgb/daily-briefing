Analysis

US stocks closed last week lower, extending a stretch of selling in AI-linked names: the S&P 500 fell 0.52%, the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.21%, and the Dow dropped 1.23%, while the VIX rose to 19.45 and the US 10-year yield ticked up to 4.99%. Oil fell more than 2% on concerns about further attacks on Saudi energy infrastructure weighed against demand destruction, with Brent and WTI both lower again this morning.

In Asia, the Nikkei 225 eased 0.35% and the Shanghai Composite was little changed, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng gained 0.49%. European benchmarks - the DAX, FTSE 100 and Euro Stoxx 50 - closed last week lower, but US futures point to a firmer open, with S&P 500 futures up 0.25% and Nasdaq 100 futures up 0.21%.

This week's focus is the Federal Reserve's policy decision on Wednesday and megacap earnings from Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon, seen as a test for the AI trade after last week's pullback. Brazilian assets are expected to come under pressure after Lula's re-election, and the UK Budget on October 28 is also in view.

World news

Geopolitics

Brazil's Lula wins historic fourth term

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva defeated right-wing challenger Flávio Bolsonaro in Brazil's presidential runoff, winning 51.76% of the vote after trailing in the first round. Bolsonaro has refused to concede and is seeking to have the result annulled. The win ends a string of right-wing victories across Latin America. Investors expect Brazilian stocks and the real to fall in reaction to the result.

Why it matters: The outcome reshapes the political balance in Latin America and is being read in Washington as a setback for US influence in the region, while markets brace for volatility in Brazilian assets.

Sources: CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Politics · NYT World · FT World · Reuters · Bloomberg Markets · AP News · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Markets · NPR World · Guardian World · Reuters

Serbia's Vucic concedes after student-led movement wins election

Aleksandar Vucic, Serbia's leader for 12 years, conceded defeat in a snap parliamentary election after a student-led protest movement won a majority. Turnout was close to 70%, the highest since Yugoslavia's breakup in 1992. The movement grew out of nearly two years of anti-corruption demonstrations.

Why it matters: The result ends over a decade of Vucic's rule and could shift Serbia's foreign policy, including its relations with the EU.

Sources: AP News · CNBC Top News · Reuters · DW · FT World · NYT World · BBC World · Guardian World · Bloomberg Politics

Venezuelan opposition leader Machado returns from exile

María Corina Machado, the Nobel Peace Prize-winning opposition leader, returned to Venezuela after nearly 10 months in exile, drawing large crowds in Caracas. Her return comes about a month after the US government seized President Nicolás Maduro. She is pressing the interim leadership for new elections.

Why it matters: Her return raises the stakes for Venezuela's political transition and could affect whether and when new elections take place.

Sources: AP News · Al Jazeera · France 24 · CNBC Top News · BBC World · NPR World · FT World · Guardian World · NYT World · Bloomberg Politics · Reuters

Israel heads into a pivotal election

Israel is holding what is described as one of its most important elections in its history, after three years of regional war. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu faces former general Gadi Eisenkot as his main challenger, in a vote centered on security and democracy. Israeli media have reported friction between Netanyahu and his military chief over Iran policy just before the vote.

Why it matters: The outcome will shape Israel's approach to Iran and Gaza, and its relations with Washington, at a sensitive regional moment.

Sources: NYT World · Al Jazeera · FT World · NYT World · Al Jazeera · France 24 · BBC World · BBC World · NPR World

France's unrest continues as Le Pen launches 2027 campaign

Protests over inequality and living costs drew an estimated 410,000 people across France over the weekend, with extra security deployed to avoid a repeat of earlier looting. Marine Le Pen used a rally in Orléans to formally launch her presidential campaign, pledging to revive oil prospecting. The unrest has already pushed French bond yields to a 24-year high.

Why it matters: Continued instability and a strengthened far-right challenger add to uncertainty around France's budget and borrowing costs.

Sources: CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Markets · Reuters · Guardian World · France 24 · Reuters · France 24

China's Communist Party opens fifth plenum

China's ruling Communist Party opened its fifth plenum, a closed-door meeting of top officials running from October 26 to 29, expected to focus on strengthening party control and ideology, including Xi Jinping's anti-corruption campaign. Xi is believed to be preparing to secure a fourth term in 2027. Analysts have questioned whether the long-running purges are about corruption or about loyalty.

Why it matters: The plenum's decisions on governance and economic direction will shape policy signals for the world's second-largest economy.

Sources: NYT World · Reuters · Guardian World · Bloomberg Politics

Economy & Policy

Oil falls as Saudi attack risk and demand concerns weigh

Oil prices fell more than 2% as traders weighed the risk of further attacks on Saudi Arabian energy infrastructure against concerns that elevated prices could erode demand. Saudi Arabia said it intercepted missiles and drones from Iran-backed Houthi forces, who claimed separate strikes on energy sites including East-West oil pumping stations. Saudi Arabia reopened Riyadh's airport despite continuing Houthi threats and said it would store oil in Singapore to bolster Asian supply security.

Why it matters: Continued attacks on Gulf energy infrastructure keep a geopolitical risk premium in oil markets even as prices fall on demand worries.

Sources: CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · Al Jazeera · Reuters · Reuters · Reuters · NYT World

Fed decision and megacap earnings headline a pivotal market week

US stock futures rose as traders looked to recover from the S&P 500's losing week, with the Federal Reserve's policy decision due Wednesday. Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon are among roughly 160 S&P 500 companies reporting this week, seen as a critical test for the artificial intelligence trade after a recent pullback in AI-linked stocks. Copper extended last week's decline as AI stocks came under renewed pressure ahead of the Fed meeting.

Why it matters: The combination of a Fed decision and megacap tech earnings could determine whether the recent AI-stock selloff stabilizes or deepens.

Sources: CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Markets · CNBC Top News

UK Budget preview: investors want 'do no harm' from Healey

Chancellor John Healey will present the UK Budget on October 28, with large bond investors reportedly hoping for reassurance rather than changes to fiscal rules. Healey is separately reported to be preparing to roughly double spending on ending rough sleeping, with an extra £432 million allocated. A cabinet split has also emerged over the Rosebank North Sea oilfield decision.

Why it matters: How the Budget balances new spending against fiscal credibility will be watched closely by gilt investors already wary of UK debt levels.

Sources: FT World · BBC Business · BBC Business · FT World · Reuters · Guardian World

Business & Tech

SpaceX closes $40 billion debt deal for AI chips

Elon Musk said SpaceX has closed $40 billion in debt financing to purchase GPUs, as the company expands an AI business that rents out access to advanced chips. Musk predicted a shakeout among so-called neocloud providers. The financing adds to a wave of AI-infrastructure investment, alongside Tesla's own AI ambitions tied to a possible SpaceX merger.

Why it matters: Large debt-funded bets on AI infrastructure raise questions about financing risk if the growth in AI spending slows.

Sources: CNBC Top News · MarketWatch · Reuters · FT World
