Analysis

US stocks fell on Tuesday as hotter-than-expected October inflation data reinforced fears that the Federal Reserve may need to raise rates further, pushing the 10-year Treasury yield to 4.98%. The S&P 500 lost 0.38%, the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.74% and the Dow dropped 0.30%, with Nvidia extending its slide for a second session amid the fallout from CEO Jensen Huang's alleged affair with a suspected Chinese spy. The VIX eased slightly to 19.46 even as the inflation print and ongoing Nvidia turmoil weighed on sentiment.

Asian markets extended their rally into a third day, led by the Nikkei 225's 1.43% gain, with the Shanghai Composite up 0.60%, even as the US inflation data spilled over into a selloff in Japanese government bonds. The Hang Seng slipped 0.64%. European markets were mixed in Tuesday trading, with the DAX up 0.31% and the Euro Stoxx 50 up 0.59%, while the FTSE 100 fell 0.29%. US futures point higher, with S&P 500 futures up 0.61% and Nasdaq 100 futures up 1.44%, as markets try to recover from the Nvidia-led decline.

No major economic data or watch-list earnings are scheduled in Europe today, leaving focus on Anthropic's IPO pricing process, which is targeting a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion, and on further Fed commentary following the inflation report. The Nvidia situation remains in play as the company's internal investigation and a shareholder lawsuit proceed. Elsewhere, gold rose 0.94% and oil firmed, while Bitcoin fell 1.85% and the dollar index was little changed.

Geopolitics

Russia and Ukraine hold second day of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi

Russia and Ukraine held a second day of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi, with Washington calling for a "new" security framework. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said he was pleased with changes in the US mediators' approach and described the first day as constructive. Ukraine separately said it captured its first Indonesian prisoner of war, pointing to a reported surge in Russian recruitment from Indonesia.

Why it matters: Progress or failure in these talks will shape the near-term direction of the war and broader European security arrangements.

Sources: AP News · NPR World · Guardian World · France 24 · Reuters · BBC World

House Republicans block effort to impeach Defense Secretary Hegseth

House Republicans blocked an effort by Republican Rep. Thomas Massie to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth over US military action in the Middle East, days after a difficult midterm election for the GOP. President Trump praised the outcome, while Democratic Senator Ruben Gallego said there were "good grounds" for impeachment and that Hegseth could still face scrutiny. Eleven Democrats were absent for the vote.

Why it matters: The vote signals continued Republican support for the administration's military actions despite midterm losses, leaving oversight of the Iran war unresolved for now.

Sources: AP News · Al Jazeera · BBC World · Guardian World · DW · Al Jazeera · Bloomberg Politics · Bloomberg Politics

US Army soldier assigned to NATO unit arrested in UK on spying suspicion

A 41-year-old US Army soldier assigned to a NATO unit was arrested in Britain on suspicion of espionage linked to Russia. The senior soldier was detained near Gloucester in western England and is being questioned in London on national security offences.

Why it matters: An espionage case involving a NATO-assigned US soldier raises concerns about intelligence security within Western military alliances amid heightened tensions with Russia.

Sources: BBC World · NYT World · AP News · France 24

US charges three Iranian operatives over UK airbase plot; Bessent moved for safety

The US Justice Department charged three men described as Iranian operatives over an alleged plot to attack the RAF Fairford airbase in the UK, which is used by US forces. Separately, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was reportedly moved to a military base after threats on his life linked to Iran, following the indictment of the three Iranian nationals on terrorism-related charges.

Why it matters: The charges highlight security threats tied to the Iran conflict extending beyond the Middle East into Europe and the top levels of the US government.

Economy & Policy

Hot US inflation fuels Fed rate-hike bets, pushing yields toward 5%

US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October, a hotter-than-expected reading that was still a slight improvement from September. The data, combined with comments from Fed officials including Governor Waller, pushed the 10-year Treasury yield back toward 5% and fueled bets that the Federal Reserve may need to raise rates more aggressively. US stocks fell on the news, and the selloff spilled over into Japanese government bonds overnight.

Why it matters: A hawkish shift in rate expectations raises borrowing costs across the economy and has already pressured equity and bond markets in the US and Asia.

Sources: CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Markets · Reuters · Reuters · MarketWatch · Reuters

Bessent says US and China should stay 'clear-eyed' on AI cooperation

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the US and China should "harbor no illusions" as they cooperate and compete on AI, following a September agreement between Washington and Beijing to launch an AI dialogue. Bessent and US Ambassador to China David Perdue both said the US aims to "de-risk," not decouple, from China, describing relations as currently "bumpy" ahead of President Trump's planned visit to China.

Why it matters: The tone of US-China engagement on AI and trade will influence technology supply chains and could reduce the risk of sudden escalation in the relationship.

Sources: CNBC Top News · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Markets · Reuters

China's trade surplus passes $1 trillion at record pace single-source report

China's trade surplus passed $1 trillion at a record pace, as the country's factories continue to find eager buyers worldwide. The export boom is raising concerns among trading partner nations about its impact on their own industries.

Why it matters: A record trade surplus intensifies trade tensions with partners already wary of Chinese export competition, including in Europe and the US.

Sources: NYT World

Business & Tech

Nvidia CEO's spy scandal widens into lawsuits and political fallout

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang faces a widening scandal after reports that he had an affair with a woman identified by US intelligence as a Chinese spy, with ties to him dating to at least 2023. Nvidia has hired a law firm to investigate, a Republican senator on the Senate banking committee has called for Huang to resign, and the company and Huang face a shareholder lawsuit alleging investors were misled. Nvidia shares fell for a second straight session, and US lawmakers are pushing for tighter chip export rules toward China in response.

Why it matters: Nvidia sits at the center of the AI trade and global chip supply chains, so a prolonged leadership crisis or tighter export controls could ripple through AI-related stocks and US-China tech policy.

Sources: AP News · BBC World · BBC World · CNBC Top News · FT World · Reuters · NYT Business · AP News · MarketWatch · NYT Business · Reuters

Anthropic targets up to $1.4 trillion valuation in blockbuster IPO

Anthropic is targeting a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion for its IPO, offering 825 million shares priced between $70 and $78, in what would be one of the largest public listings ever. Nvidia plans to invest $10 billion in Anthropic alongside the offering, tying the two companies closer together. Commentators are split: CNBC's Jim Cramer called the deal bullish for the AI trade, while one venture investor questioned why Anthropic is going public given its large cash reserves. The IPO comes as investors already question the sustainability of the AI boom.

Why it matters: The size and pricing of the IPO will be read as a gauge of investor appetite for AI-related risk after a volatile stretch for AI stocks.

Sources: FT World · FT Markets · FT World · CNBC Top News · CNBC Top News · NYT Business · Bloomberg Politics · Bloomberg Markets · Bloomberg Politics · Bloomberg Politics · NYT Business · France 24

CoreWeave beats estimates while SoftBank reports $7 billion loss

CoreWeave shares rose after the company beat revenue expectations, raised guidance and announced $9 billion in planned prepayments, easing concerns about a slowdown in AI infrastructure demand; it targets profitability by 2028. SoftBank shares fluctuated after reporting a $7 billion quarterly net loss, including a $3.7 billion writedown tied to its US data centers.

Why it matters: The mixed results show investors are scrutinizing AI infrastructure spending closely, with some companies reassuring markets while others show the costs of the AI buildout.
